CODEBREAKERS · FOR TEACHERS

The Hidden War

A WWII intelligence game for high school history. Each student plays one of five intelligence roles across six rounds and a short 1945 epilogue (1939–1945). In each round they crack a short cipher, make a decision, and see what really happened. Then they swap Signal IDs with classmates on Padlet to rebuild six secret messages, which they hand in to you privately.

A SIMPLE PLAN

  1. Before class: make your Padlet, and decide how students hand in work privately.
  2. Optional, the mini lesson: it isn't needed to play. It gives more context for the game if your students aren't familiar with the tradecraft of WWII: codebreaking, spies, double agents and deception.
  3. Play: show the class briefing, then students play all six rounds.
  4. Decode: students post their cards, decode classmates, file verdicts and submit.
  5. After: paste the edio submissions into Teacher Check to see who is done. Showing the class the Unmasking is optional.

BEFORE CLASS

  1. Make your class Padlet in one click: open the ready-made Padlet while signed in to Padlet. It makes your own copy, with a column for each role, the pinned welcome post and an example card. None of another class’s posts come with it.
  2. Check the welcome post’s last step. It tells students to submit their decoded messages on edio. Edit it if your school uses a different system.
  3. Check two settings in your copy: Attribution (author names) is on, and students must sign in with their school accounts. Then every card shows who posted it, even if a student types a codename. Comments (replies) should be on too.
  4. Prefer to build your own? Any Wall or Shelf Padlet works. A suggested welcome post is below.
  5. Decide how students hand in their submission: your learning management system, a form, or email. It must be private. Never on Padlet.

Suggested welcome post (copy and edit):

Welcome, codebreakers.
1. Play the game: choose any role and finish all six rounds. Then choose, in secret, loyal agent or double agent. Tell no one.
2. Post ONLY your case card here. In the game, click Copy card and text for Padlet. Then click + in your role's column and paste twice: the first paste adds your card image, then click in the text box and paste again for your post text.
3. Decode classmates from the OTHER four roles: copy their whole Padlet post and paste it into the game's Decoder board. Then click Trust or Traitor. For your first 2 classmates, that one click also copies your reply. The game's Decoder board shows which roles you still need.
4. Reply to 2 classmates: paste the reply as a comment under their card. It starts with DECODED, their ID, your PROOF code and your SEAL (your secret verdict). Never say whether you trust them.
5. When all six messages are decoded, turn in your submission on edio. Never post the messages here.

IN CLASS

  1. Students choose their own role and type a name or codename. Every role has a different piece of each message, so a mix of roles across the class is what makes the puzzle work. If one role is missing, students cannot finish; nudge a few students toward it.
  2. They play six rounds. Each round has a briefing, a cipher to crack with the dial, a decision, and a debrief comparing their choice with what really happened. Plan for at least one class period. A Read aloud button at the top of every screen (and in the Archive) reads the text out loud using the computer's built-in voice; headphones help. It never reads blacked-out Archive text, and nothing is sent online.
  3. They post their card on Padlet: a card image plus the post text, both copied with one button (Copy card and text for Padlet; in Padlet the first paste adds the card and a second paste adds the text; separate copy and download buttons are folded underneath), which includes their Signal ID and their classified intel reports (no questions, so posts stay short).
  4. They decode classmates. On the Decoder board they paste a classmate's whole Padlet post (or type the Signal ID) for cards of the four other roles; the game finds the Signal ID. Clicking Trust or Traitor seals their verdict and copies the reply in one step, so each classmate is three steps: copy the post, click, paste the reply. Students reply to 2 of the classmates they decode (they still decode all four other roles for the messages); after 2 replies, the click only seals the verdict and a reply is optional. Above the decoder, a Still needed line names the roles they are missing (each role has its own Padlet column) and counts their replies (for example "Replies: 1 of 2 done"). The reply is about a round where the two students chose differently, with that round's question; students can change both if they want. Each one shows that student's choices and unlocks their pieces of the messages. They reply to 2 cards with the reply text the game writes. Each reply carries a proof code (such as R-7KQ) made from the student's own Signal ID and the classmate's, so you can see who really replied.
  5. They hand in the six messages. When all six are complete, the game makes a submission with their name, their own Signal ID, the Signal IDs they decoded, the proof codes of their replies, their verdicts, and a short reflection: they choose the message that surprised them most and a reason from a list (typing their own words is optional). They copy it and hand it in privately.

TRUST OR TRAITOR

Trust or Traitor is about reading each other, not quizzing. Students make a quick call on each classmate they decode, and find out later who trusted them. Choices only, no typing, and no extra decoding.

  1. A secret choice. After round 6, each student chooses in secret to be a loyal agent or a double agent. A double agent picks one of three intel reports to falsify, then chooses its lie from two false versions (no typing). The choice is hidden inside their Signal ID. Nobody else can see it.
  2. Classified intel reports. Each student has three short intel reports from their front. On Padlet they show only as black bars; a classmate reads them by decoding the Signal ID. If the student is a double agent, one report contains the lie.
  3. One click: Trust or Traitor. When a student decodes a classmate, they read the three reports and click Trust or Traitor. The click seals the verdict (it can't be changed) and, for their first 2 classmates, copies their reply, ready to paste on the Padlet.
  4. A judgment call, with an optional source check. Students can decide from the three reports in front of them, the way real intelligence officers often had to, with no way to check. Nobody has to decode extra cards.
  5. Bonus: check a report against the Archive (source analysis). Before deciding, a student may check one report against the Archive file it comes from. The game opens that file (key details still behind black bars to reveal), and the student marks the report Backed up, Proven wrong or Can't tell. Only one check is allowed per classmate, so students still have to judge which report looks suspicious. The check is sealed with the verdict: on the Padlet nobody can see it, but the card's owner sees in Who trusted you? which classmates checked which report and what they found, and how many went with their gut. Once all six messages are decoded, students also see whether each of their checks was right. Corroborating a claim against a second source, and reading closely for the detail that matters, is the skill practised here. Some files also mention one of the false versions in another context (Tripoli, Sicily), so careful reading counts.
  6. Bonus clue from teammates. If a student happens to have decoded two agents from the same role anyway, the game shows which reports differ.
  7. The sealed verdict. The reply the game writes for the Padlet carries a SEAL code. Classmates reading the Padlet can't tell what it means. The card's owner pastes the replies into Who trusted you? on their Decoder board to see how many trusted them, or how many they fooled. This gives students a reason to come back and read the replies on their card, even if they play at different times. It is optional and never affects their submission.
  8. The Archive. Background files for every role, filed in drawers, with key details behind black bars that students hover over or tap to reveal. The files are reconstructions: the facts are checked, but the layout imitates a wartime file. Nobody needs it to make a verdict, but it is the source for the optional report check.
  9. Results. Students see whether their own verdicts were right once they have decoded all six messages.

The Unmasking (optional). In Teacher Check, under the table, press Show the Unmasking. It shows who was a double agent, the best deceiver, the best spy-catcher, and every planted lie with the truth beside it. Keep it hidden until everyone has finished.

GRADING

  1. Collect the submissions and paste them, all at once, into Teacher Check.
  2. Each student gets one short row. Done? means every step is complete, and a Steps checklist shows each one: card posted on the Padlet, all four other roles decoded, at least 2 replies, all six messages right, and a reflection. Anything missing is listed under Done? as Skipped. The card and the replies are checked on the Padlet itself once you add the Padlet's export (below); without it the card shows as ? and replies are counted from the proof codes in the submission. The row also shows how many classmates decoded them, and a note if something needs a look (a made-up Signal ID, two students with the same Signal ID, the optional own-words part of a reflection copied word for word (chosen reflections are expected to match), a reply code that isn't theirs).
  3. Full details, Trust or Traitor and the Unmasking open with one click under the table, if you want them. You can skip them entirely.
  4. Checking replies (optional, no searching): in Padlet, open Share, then Export, and save the board as a spreadsheet (Excel) or CSV. In Teacher Check, choose that file under Replies on the Padlet. The file is read on your computer only. The Replies column then counts each student's replies really found on the Padlet, matched by proof code, and says if a reply listed in a submission is missing. You can also paste text copied from the Padlet instead of a file. Without a file, the column counts the proof codes in each submission, and you can still search the Padlet for one (Ctrl+F).
  5. Use Copy for a spreadsheet to paste the short table anywhere you like. Look up one Signal ID shows the choices behind any card on the Padlet.

Known limits: Signal IDs are checked, but they are not tamper-proof, and a Signal ID from another class works in any class. Teacher Check flags the most common shortcuts; it does not replace your judgment.
